## Principles

- Everything in `media-src/originals/` is a byte-for-byte copy of the files you uploaded. It is never modified. SHA-256 checksums are in `docs/MEDIA-CHECKSUMS.txt`, and the copies were verified identical to a fresh extraction of your uploaded ZIP.

- No upscaling. No cropping of files. No colour grading, filters or retouching. No blending of photographs into backgrounds.

### How fidelity was checked

1. SSIM computed separately on the R, G and B channels over the lit parts of the picture (a black background would otherwise inflate the score). My first version of this check used luminance only and was too lenient about colour; it was replaced.
2. An independent whole-image RGB SSIM from ffmpeg (`ffmpeg -i decoded-avif.png -i original.png -lavfi ssim -f null -`). It is stricter on near-black gradients, and it is the reason six images were left as JPEG.
3. 100% and 2x side-by-side crops of p5 (dark floor gradient and the face). No banding or colour shift was visible.

SSIM is a screening tool, not proof. Because the originals are small JPEGs (30 to 150 KB each), AVIF saves little in absolute terms (about 8 to 21 KB per image), so the rules favour keeping your original pixels.

### Audio measurements (ffmpeg, software only; I have not listened)

The terms:

- **Loudness** (LUFS) is how loud it sounds on average over time.
- **Sample peak** is the largest individual sample value after decoding. Above 0 dBFS means the decoded signal exceeds the maximum that a standard 16-bit output can represent.
- **True peak** (dBTP) estimates the peak of the continuous waveform between samples. It can be higher than the sample peak.

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Integrated loudness (EBU R128) | -12.5 LUFS |
| Loudness range | 15.3 LU |
| True peak (ffmpeg `ebur128`) | +5.7 dBTP |
| Sample peak, left channel | 1.91 x full scale = +5.62 dBFS; 840 samples above full scale |
| Sample peak, right channel | 1.74 x full scale = +4.79 dBFS; 467 samples above full scale |
| Total samples per channel | 3,172,288 |

**Correction to an earlier message.** I previously reported a sample peak of +7.2 dBFS with 5,482 samples above full scale. That figure came from an ffmpeg mono downmix, which inflated the values. The per-channel numbers above are the correct ones. An even earlier "+1.5 dBTP" could not be reproduced and should be disregarded.

Both peak measures are above the -1 dBTP limit. Bringing the **left-channel sample peak** to -1.0 dBFS needs about 6.6 dB of gain reduction; the true peak needs about 6.7 dB. Loudness would fall from -12.5 to about -19.2 LUFS (quieter than typical web audio, which is why a comparison is provided rather than a decision).

**What gain reduction can and cannot fix.** It scales the whole signal down, so peaks stop exceeding full scale and a player no longer has to clip them at playback. It cannot repair distortion that is already in the file. If the source was clipped before being encoded, lowering the gain makes it quieter but not cleaner. Only listening can tell whether the loudest passages are audibly distorted.

Where to listen on the **original** (loudest 0.5 s windows by measured level, mono average): 60.0 to 61.0 s (-11.0 and -12.6 dBFS RMS), 39.0 s, 34.5 s and 31.0 s (about -12 dBFS RMS).
